4. Fundamentals of Security

Security can be about finding the right balance between security and user accessibility

  • users will find their way around annoying security practices, causing a bigger issue than a less secure option
  • Must be easy enough for some idiot user to use the fucking thing, but hard enough to prevent malicious activity

Information Security - Act of protecting data and information from unauthorized access, unlawful modification and disruption, disclosure and corruption, and destruction

  • The data itself, not the device

Information System Security - Act of protecting the systems that hold and process the critical data

  • The inverse of Information Security

CIA Triad

Confidentiality ensures that information or data is only accessible by authorized users

  • Only those approved can access a share drive Integrity ensures that data remains accurate and unaltered unless modification is required
  • Commonly used documents across multiple LOBs shouldn’t be altered by just anyone and is resilient to data corruption or loss Availability ensures that data and resources are accessible and functional when needed by authorized users
  • Websites stay online and up
CIANA Pentagon (CIA Triad ++)

AAA of Security

Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of a user or system

  • Credentials, 2FA, etc. Authorization defines what actions or resources a user can access
  • Read, Write, Execute permissions
  • Command/Application permissions Accounting is the act of tracking user activities and resource usage, typically for audit or bulling purposes
  • Logs for monitoring for unusual behavior

Security Controls

Measures or mechanisms put in place to mitigate risks and protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information systems and data

  • Technical
  • Managerial
  • Operational
  • Physical

We do also have more definitions of specific types of security controls

  • Preventative
    • Firewall to prevent intrusion
  • Deterrent
    • Active monitoring to deter intrusion
  • Detective
    • Active monitoring
  • Corrective
    • Incident response
  • Compensating
    • A management, operational, and/or technical control (i.e., safeguard or countermeasure) employed by an organization in lieu of a recommended security control in the low, moderate, or high baselines that provides equivalent or comparable protection for an information system.
  • Directive
    • Policies and procedures that dictate specific actions or behaviors by users or systems
    • Mandatory password resets

Zero Trust

Zero trust is a security model that operates on the principle that no one, inside or out of the organization, should be trusted by default. In order to implement Zero Trust, we need two planes of operation.

  1. Control Plane consists of the adaptive identity, threat scope reduction, policy-driven access control, and secured zones
  2. Data Plane is focused on the subject/system, policy engine, policy administrator, and establishing policy enforcement points

5. Threats and Vulnerabilities

As cysec professionals cannot control every threat out there, but we can minimize their damage by identifying and fixing vulnerabilities.

  • Risk management. Find the right balance!
  • Most vulnerabilities we have power over as they’re typically created by internal factors
    • Example: Driving to work. Car can break down, might forget it’s “your day” to drive the kids to school, etc.
  • Most threats are outside of our control ; Outside actors
    • You can’t control a drunk driver or natural disasters

  • Example: Leave early for work so that if there is an earthquake or accident, then you will have buffer time to change route

Risk Management is the systematic process of identifying, assessing, and mitigating threats or uncertainties that can affect the organization. It typically involves analyzing the risks’ liklihood and impact, developing strategies to minimizing harm, and monitoring measures’ effectiveness.

10. Authentication

Ensures individuals or entities are who they claim to be.

Authentication Methods:

  1. Something you know (Knowledge Factor)
    • Things like a code or password
  2. Something you have (Possession Factor)
    • Badge, Yubikey, Text passcode
  3. Something you are (Inherence Factor)
    • Biometric authentication,
  4. Something you do (Action Factor)
    • Secret handshake, handwriting samples, walking patterns tracked by computers
  5. Somewhere you are (Location Factor)
    • Relies on the user being in a geographic location for access; Country, building, 20-foot perimeter, etc.

You can combine two or more of these to create a more secure method of authentication. Typically known as MFA or Multi-factor Authentication. Commonly 2FA is how many services run. Send a text and gotta know the password.

15. Zero Trust

Before, security was conducted like a castle. Strong walls, free access once inside.

Zero trust adds security to inside the castle. Trust nothing and verify everything. Demand verification for every device, user, transaction, regardless of origin.

Zero trust planes:

  1. Control Plane
    • Overarching framework and set of components responsible for defining, managing, and enforcing policies.
    1. Adaptive Identity
      • Uses adaptive identities that rely on real-time validation
    2. Threat Scope Reduction
      • Limit the user’s access to only what they need for work tasks
    3. Policy-driven Access Control
      • Developing, managing, and enforcing user access based on roles and responsibilities
    4. Secured Zones
      • Isolated environments on a network that are designed to house data.
    5. Policy Engine
      • Cross references the access request with its predefined policies
    6. Policy Administrator
      • Used to establish and manage access policies
  2. Data Plane
    1. Subject/System
      • Refers to the individual or entity attempting to gain access
    2. Policy Enforcement Point
      • Allow or restrict access. Acts as a gatekeeper to sensitive areas of the systems or network

Objective 2.4

  1. Viruses
    1. Boot Sector Viruses are stored in the boot partition of your disk and start on bootup and typical AVs struggle with detecting these. Need a boot partition antivirus
    2. Macro Viruses are typically embedded in files that can execute macros
    3. Program viruses try to find application files to infect with malicious code
    4. Multipartite Viruses are a combination of a boot sector and program viruses. Hide on boot, install after boot
    5. Encrypted viruses evade antivirus detection by using encryption
    6. Polymorphic viruses are more advanced than encrypted viruses. The code is changed each time it’s executed by altering the decryption module
    7. Metamorphic viruses are even more advanced than polymorphic. They’re able to rewrite itself entirely before it attempts to infect a given file
    8. Stealth viruses refer to a type of technique. Think any virus that tries to hide itself from the AV
    9. Armored viruses have a layer of protection to confuse a program or person who’s trying to analyze it

  1. Incident Response Process
    1. 7-phase incident response model
      1. Preparation - Strengthen the systems/networks against attacks
      2. Detection - Identify the security incident
      3. Analysis - Thorough examination and evaluation of the incident
      4. Containment - Limit the incident’s impact; Secure data and protect business operations

        1. Known-good backup
        2. Security patches
        3. Configuration updates
      7. Post-incident activity/Lessons learned - Look for improvements in what you just did. How could you have done that better?

    1. Create a hypothesis - What would do us the most harm?
    2. Profile the threat actors and their activities - Who would want to harm us?
    3. Assume the current rules haven’t flagged the threats

      2. focus on what bypasses existing rules
      3. Explore cases where queries don’t yield expected data
  3. Root Cause Analysis - NO BLAMING ANYONE, purely forward moving
    1. Define the scope of the incident - Who and what all is affected?
    2. Determine the casual relationships - What caused to this incident?
    3. Identify an effective solution
    4. Implement and track the solution
  4. Incident Response Training and Testing
    1. Training =/= Testing
    2. Training teaches the theory
    3. Testing trains the “muscle”

    4. Reporting
